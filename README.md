# Graduate Student 


### Research Abstract 
I am a Ph.D. Materials Scientist specializing in semiconductor process development, advanced surface characterization, and nanoscale materials engineering. My research bridges chemistry and semiconductor manufacturing through area-selective deposition, self-assembled monolayers, carbon nanotube integration, and chemical metrology. I enjoy transforming scientific ideas into scalable technologies for next-generation electronics.

#### Technical Skills: 
- Surface Characterization: AFM, AFM IR, O-PTIR, FTIR, Raman, XPS, SEM, XRD, UV Vis, Spectroscopic Ellipsometry
- Process Development: ALD, Area Selective ALD, Flowable CVD, SAMs, Thin Film Engineering
- Programming: MATLAB, JMP, OriginPro, ImageJ, CasaXPS, Gwyddion, SOLIDWORKS

## Projects
### Three-dimensional NiCoP hollow spheres: an efficient electrode material for hydrogen evolution reaction and supercapacitor applications @ NISER
- Designed and characterized 3D NiCoP hollow nanostructures via hydrothermal process and XRD, enabling high surface-area architectures for electrochemical applications.
- Demonstrated HER performance (≈ 160 mV at 10 mA cm⁻²) with 18 hours of operation stability.
- Engineered supercapacitor electrodes delivering ~960 F g⁻¹ specific capacitance and ~95% capacity retention after 10,000 cycles.


### Spatially Resolved Spectroscopic Investigation of Tau Protein Phase Separation in Alzheimer's Disease @ UA 
- Investigated how liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) influences tau protein aggregation by combining AFM-IR and OPTIR spectroscopy to monitor nanoscale structural evolution and secondary structure changes within protein condensates.
- Identified experimental conditions that promote tau condensate formation and established a spectroscopic framework for correlating droplet morphology with aggregation pathways, providing mechanistic insights into Alzheimer's disease and a broadly applicable methodology for studying other neurodegenerative proteins.
